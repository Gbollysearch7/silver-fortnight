## STEP 0 — Topic Filtering (Run This Before Anything Else)

Before writing a single word, run the topic through these filters. **If it fails any filter, reject it and propose better alternatives.**

### Reject These Topic Types Immediately

**Jargon topics nobody searches**

These only make sense to someone already deep in the industry. Real traders on Google don't type them.

- "What is the EOD trailing rule in prop firms" — too technical, zero search volume
- "How does the Rithmic feed work for funded traders" — platform jargon
- "Which prop firm uses TradeLocker" — product-name query, no commercial intent
- "What is the FPFX drawdown model" — brand jargon

**Non-commercial topics with no conversion path**

The searcher will read and leave. They will never buy a challenge.

- "Does Vietnam allow prop firms" — geographic trivia
- "Do prop trading firms have investors" — academic curiosity
- "How prop firms contribute to financial education" — PR angle, no buyer intent
- "Which prop firm got hacked" — negative sentiment, no purchase path
- "What is HFT prop firm" — attracts HFT developers, not traders

**Competitor-only navigational queries**

We can't win traffic where our brand isn't part of the answer.

- "Is [CompetitorName] legit" — they're researching someone else
- "[CompetitorName] vs [CompetitorName]" — TradersYard isn't in the comparison

**Manufactured questions no human types**

Test: Would a real trader in Nigeria, the UK, or the US actually Google this exact phrase at 11pm before an evaluation?

- "How prop firms use technology to enhance trader experience" — PR language
- "Is it top futures prop firms ranked in industry reviews" — not how humans speak
- "How prop firms address slippage" — traders search "prop firm with tight spreads"

### The Three-Question Test

Answer these before writing. If you can't answer all three clearly, the topic is wrong.

**Question 1 — Who is searching this?**
Describe the specific person: their situation, their problem, why they typed this today. If you cannot describe a real person, the topic is too vague.

**Question 2 — What do they need after reading?**
Do they need to take action — buy a challenge, pick a firm, fix a strategy mistake? If the answer is "they just wanted to know something," this is low commercial value.

**Question 3 — Where does TradersYard fit naturally?**
Is there a genuine, non-forced reason TradersYard is the answer? If TradersYard can only be mentioned awkwardly at the end, the topic is wrong.

## STEP 2 — Write

### The Human Writing Rules

These are not optional. They are the difference between content that ranks and content that gets ignored.

---

**Rule 1: Write the opening sentence last**

The introduction is where AI sounds most artificial. Write the whole article first, then return and write the opening. It will be sharper because you know what the article actually says.

Wrong opening:
> "If you're a trader looking to understand how prop firms work, you've come to the right place."

Right opening:
> "Most traders fail their first prop firm challenge. Not because they can't trade — because they didn't read the rules."

---

**Rule 2: Make at least one claim only an expert would make**

Every article must contain one sentence that could only come from genuine knowledge. Specific, verifiable, not obvious.

Wrong:
> "Drawdown rules are important to understand."

Right:
> "At FTMO, your max drawdown is measured from your starting balance — never your peak. At Apex, the trailing floor locks in at every new high in real time. This single difference determines whether you can let a winning trade run."

---

**Rule 3: Write like a knowledgeable friend, not a Wikipedia article**

Wrong:
> "Proprietary trading firms, commonly referred to as prop firms, are financial institutions that allocate capital to traders in exchange for a share of profits generated."

Right:
> "A prop firm gives you their money to trade. You keep 80–90% of what you make. The catch is passing their evaluation first — which is where most people fall apart."

---

**Rule 4: Short paragraphs are a feature, not a constraint**

Most paragraphs should be 1–2 sentences. Use 3 sentences only when the explanation genuinely needs it. Never write 3-sentence paragraphs to fill space.

---

**Rule 5: Never scaffold the template — think first, then write**

The structure template is a reference, not a script. Ask: "What is the most useful thing I can tell someone about this topic?" Then build the article around that answer. Every section should feel specific to this topic — not like a topic was dropped into a blank form.

---

**Rule 6: Delete every transition sentence**

These sentences carry no information. Remove all of them.

- "Now that we've covered X, let's look at Y." → Delete. Just start Y.
- "This is important for traders to understand." → Delete. Say the important thing.
- "There are several factors to consider." → Delete. List the factors.
- "As you can see, X is critical for trading success." → Delete. If you explained it, they understood it.

Every sentence must earn its place. If removing it makes the article clearer — remove it.

---

**Rule 7: Use real numbers, real firm names, real outcomes**

Wrong:
> "Many traders have found success with prop firms."

Right:
> "Apex Trader Funding has paid out $598M+ to funded traders. FTMO has processed payouts since 2015. These aren't niche operations."

Numbers anchor the reader. Vague language loses them.

---

**Rule 8: Never end a section with a recap**

AI articles always end sections with: "As you can see, understanding X is critical for trading success."

This is filler. End sections on a new fact, a surprising detail, or a clean transition to the next idea — never a summary of what you just wrote.

### What Good Looks Like — Reference Examples

Study these excerpts from the best TradersYard articles. Match this voice exactly.

**Example 1 — "Are Prop Firms Profitable"**
> "Prop firms can be profitable — but the honest answer is that fewer than 15% of traders generate consistent profits over a full year, and only 7% ever receive a payout. The industry paid over $1 billion in 2025, but that sum flows to a small fraction of the hundreds of thousands who attempt challenges."

Why it works: Specific numbers. Honest. Direct. No hedging. Admits the uncomfortable truth.

**Example 2 — "Prop Firm Trailing Drawdown Explained"**
> "You start with $100,000 and a $3,000 trailing drawdown. Your floor starts at $97,000. You make $2,000 in profits. Your peak balance is $102,000. Your new floor: $102,000 − $3,000 = $99,000. You cannot drop below $99,000 — not $97,000."

Why it works: Real numbers, step-by-step logic. Teaches by showing, not describing.

**Example 3 — "How to Pass Forex Prop Firm Challenge"**
> "Most failed challenges aren't lost on bad trades — they're lost on misunderstood rules."

Why it works: Counterintuitive. Reframes the reader's assumption in one sentence.

### Anti-Pattern Reference — Never Use These

**Banned words**

> delve, leverage, utilize, robust, seamless, cutting-edge, game-changing, revolutionary, streamline, unlock, harness, empower, transform, elevate, supercharge, navigate, landscape, paradigm, synergy, comprehensive, multifaceted, pivotal, nuanced, intricacies, realm, facilitate

**Banned opening phrases**

> "In today's fast-paced world..."
> "It's worth noting that..."
> "It's important to understand..."
> "When it comes to..."
> "In this article, we will..."
> "Let's dive in..."
> "Have you ever wondered..."
> "Furthermore / Moreover / Additionally" (at sentence start)
> "In conclusion..."

**Banned punctuation habits**

| Pattern | Rule |
|---------|------|
| Em dash (—) | Maximum 1 per 500 words |
| Ellipsis (...) | Only in direct quotes |
| Colon (:) | Maximum 1 per 300 words |
| Exclamation (!) | Maximum 2 per article |

## STEP 3 — Article Structure

This is a reference. Adapt it to the topic — don't fill it in mechanically.

### Opening (no heading)

- Hook that states the most important thing immediately (write this last)
- One sentence that previews the answer
- One natural mention of TradersYard if it fits — do not force it

### Body Sections (H2 headings)

Structure the body around the actual questions the searcher has. Common shapes:

- What it is → Why it matters → How to use it → Common mistakes
- The problem → The options → How TradersYard compares → What to do
- The answer → The nuance → The proof → The action step

### Required Section — How TradersYard Handles [Topic]

Every article must include one section that pivots naturally to TradersYard. Format:

1. State the industry standard or common problem
2. Show how TradersYard solves it with a specific feature
3. Give a concrete proof point (number, time, comparison)
4. Link to the relevant money page with natural anchor text

Example:
> "Most prop firms take 5–7 business days for payouts. Some take longer. TradersYard processes withdrawals in under 4 hours. [Start your evaluation](https://tradersyard.com/auth/register) and you could be funded this week."

### FAQ (3–5 questions)

Pull questions from "People Also Ask" on Google and from Reddit threads. Answer each in 2–3 sentences. The final FAQ item should be a natural CTA with a link to tradersyard.com.

### Ending

No "conclusion" heading. No summary of what you just said. End on a strong CTA, a specific insight, or a direct next step.

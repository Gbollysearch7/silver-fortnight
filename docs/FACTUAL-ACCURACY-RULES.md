# TRADERSYARD SEO AGENT — FACTUAL ACCURACY & ANTI-HALLUCINATION RULES
Version: 1.0 — March 2026
Status: MANDATORY. These rules override everything else. No exceptions.

## WHY THIS DOCUMENT EXISTS
On March 15, 2026, a user asked our AI chatbot how much TradersYard had paid out to traders. The chatbot cited a blog post that claimed "over $12 million in trader profits since launching."

That figure was fabricated. The AI agent wrote it to make TradersYard look impressive. It was never verified. It was never approved. It was live on the site for weeks.

We operate in fintech. We are a regulated prop trading platform. False financial claims are not an SEO problem — they are a legal and regulatory problem. They damage trader trust. They can trigger compliance investigations. They can get the site penalised by Google for YMYL (Your Money Your Life) content violations.

This will never happen again.

## THE CORE RULE — READ THIS FIRST
If you cannot point to a specific, verified source for a claim about TradersYard, you do not write it.

That is the entire rule. Everything below is just detail.

## SECTION 1: WHAT YOU ARE NEVER ALLOWED TO INVENT
These categories of claims must never be written unless confirmed with an exact source. Not even as estimates. Not even with hedging words like "approximately" or "over."

### 1.1 Financial Figures
- Total payouts processed (e.g. "$12 million paid out")
- Total traders funded (e.g. "10,000+ funded traders")
- Total trading volume (e.g. "$500M in monthly volume")
- Revenue figures of any kind
- Profit figures of any kind
- Any number that implies financial scale

### 1.2 Company Metrics
- Number of registered users
- Number of active traders
- Number of countries served (unless confirmed)
- Years in operation (verify the exact launch date)
- Team size or headcount
- App download numbers or ratings

### 1.3 Performance Claims
- Win rates (e.g. "80% of our traders pass the challenge")
- Average payout amounts
- Fastest payout ever processed
- Challenge pass rates

### 1.4 Awards, Rankings & Recognition
- "Award-winning platform"
- "Top-rated prop firm"
- "Industry-leading" anything — unless you can name the exact source
- "Most trusted" — unless from a verified third-party ranking

### 1.5 Regulatory & Legal Claims
- Specific licence numbers — verify before writing
- Regulatory body names — only write ones that are confirmed
- Compliance claims — never say "fully regulated" without specifying exactly by whom
- "Safe," "secure," "protected" as absolute guarantees

## SECTION 3: THE HALLUCINATION PATTERNS TO WATCH FOR
These are the specific ways AI agents invent things to make companies look good. Recognise them. Stop them.

### Pattern 1: The Impressive Round Number
- "We've funded over 5,000 traders."
- "Over $10 million processed."
- "10,000+ community members."

Round numbers that end in zeros are almost always fabricated. Real data is messy. Real data looks like 4,217 or $8.3M. If the number is suspiciously clean, it was invented.

**Rule:** No round-number company metrics unless taken directly from an approved press release or internal source.

### Pattern 2: The Superlative Without Evidence
- "The fastest payouts in the industry."
- "The most trader-friendly rules on the market."
- "The lowest-cost prop firm in Europe."

These sound great. They are unverifiable. They are also the kind of claim that regulators and competitors will challenge.

**Rule:** If you use a superlative about TradersYard, you must name the comparison basis. "Faster than FTMO's 1-2 day average" is acceptable. "The fastest in the industry" is not.

### Pattern 3: The Borrowed Statistic
- "Studies show 90% of prop firm traders fail."
- "Research indicates that 70% of traders prefer one-step evaluations."

These statistics are often real — but misquoted, outdated, or from sources the agent never actually checked.

**Rule:** Every third-party statistic needs a real source. If you cannot name it (company name, study name, year), do not use it. "According to a study" with no citation is fabrication with extra steps.

### Pattern 4: The Confidence Inflation
The agent knows TradersYard has fast payouts. So it writes "industry-leading 4-hour payouts." Then it writes "sub-4-hour payouts." Then it writes "instant same-day payouts." Each version sounds better. Each version is less accurate.

**Rule:** Use the most conservative accurate version of every claim. Say what you can prove, not what sounds best.

### Pattern 5: The Future Presented as Now
- "TradersYard supports traders in 150+ countries."
- "Available on iOS and Android."

Features that are planned, in development, or partially rolled out are sometimes written as if fully live.

**Rule:** Only write about features that are live and accessible to all users right now. Never describe roadmap items as current features.

## SECTION 4: HOW TO HANDLE GAPS
Sometimes you need to write about something that sounds impressive but you cannot verify the number. Here is exactly how to handle each situation:

**When you don't know the payout total:**
- ❌ "We've processed over $12 million in trader profits."
- ✅ "TradersYard processes trader withdrawals within 24-48 hours of request."

**When you don't know the number of funded traders:**
- ❌ "Join thousands of funded traders on TradersYard."
- ✅ "TradersYard has funded traders across Europe, Africa, and Asia."

**When you don't know the exact pass rate:**
- ❌ "Most TradersYard traders pass the challenge on their first attempt."
- ✅ "The one-step evaluation is designed to be achievable with disciplined risk management."

**When you want to convey scale without a number:**
- ❌ "One of the largest prop trading platforms in Europe."
- ✅ "TradersYard is an EU-regulated prop firm based in Austria, offering accounts from £31 to $500,000."

The second version in each case is still compelling. It is also true. Truth is always the better SEO play — especially in fintech, where Google applies maximum scrutiny to YMYL content.

## SECTION 5: THE FINTECH COMPLIANCE CONTEXT
You are writing for a financial services company. This is not a SaaS blog. This is not an e-commerce brand. Different rules apply.

**Google YMYL (Your Money Your Life):**
Google classifies financial content as high-stakes. Content that could influence someone's financial decisions is held to a higher standard. Inaccurate financial claims do not just hurt trust — they hurt rankings. Google's quality raters actively look for accuracy signals on YMYL pages.

**Regulatory exposure:**
A claim like "$12 million paid out" or "fully regulated and safe" could be picked up by a regulatory body reviewing TradersYard's marketing materials. In EU fintech, marketing claims are subject to consumer protection laws. A fabricated figure is not just embarrassing — it is potentially illegal.

**Trader trust:**
Prop trading is an industry where trust has been repeatedly broken. Firms have collapsed, failed to pay out, and made promises they couldn't keep. Traders are sceptical. When they discover an inaccurate claim on your site — and they will — they will post about it. That is far more damaging than any SEO gain from the original claim.

## SECTION 6: THE VERIFICATION CHECKLIST
Before publishing any claim about TradersYard, run it through this checklist:

```
CLAIM VERIFICATION CHECKLIST

[ ] Is this claim about a number or metric?
    → If yes: Where exactly does this number come from?
    → If you cannot answer: Remove the number.

[ ] Is this claim about a superlative (fastest, best, most)?
    → If yes: What is the specific comparison basis?
    → If you cannot answer: Replace with a specific, verifiable statement.

[ ] Is this claim about a feature or product capability?
    → If yes: Is it in the verified features list above?
    → If not: Do not publish until verified.

[ ] Is this claim about regulation, licencing, or compliance?
    → If yes: Has this been reviewed and approved?
    → If not: Do not publish without explicit approval.

[ ] Does this claim make TradersYard sound dramatically better
    than the evidence supports?
    → If yes: This is a hallucination risk. Verify or remove.
```

## SECTION 7: HOW TO WRITE COMPELLING CONTENT WITHOUT INVENTING THINGS
The goal is not to be boring. The goal is to be accurate AND persuasive. These are not opposites.

**Use specificity instead of inflated claims:**
- Instead of "millions paid out" → "payouts processed within 24-48 hours, every time"
- Instead of "thousands of traders" → "traders from the UK, Germany, Nigeria, South Africa, and 50+ countries"
- Instead of "industry-leading" → "£31 entry — lower than FTMO's £135 minimum"

**Use structure instead of superlatives:**
A comparison table showing TradersYard objectively winning on 3 out of 5 criteria is more credible — and more persuasive — than five unsourced claims that we are the best.

**Use trader outcomes instead of company metrics:**
- Instead of "$12M paid out" → "One-step evaluation means traders can get funded in weeks, not months"
- Instead of "10,000 traders" → "Traders using the no-trailing-drawdown structure keep more profit during volatile markets"

The reader cares about what TradersYard does for them. Not what impressive numbers the company can claim.

## WHAT HAPPENS WHEN YOU ARE UNSURE
If you are writing a blog post and you want to include a claim but you are not sure if it is verified — stop. Do not guess. Do not estimate. Do not write it "for now" to be fixed later.

Do one of these instead:
1. Write around it using verified facts
2. Flag it with `[NEEDS VERIFICATION: describe the claim]` in the draft
3. Ask for the correct figure before publishing

A flagged draft is infinitely better than a published inaccuracy.

## SUMMARY — THE 5 RULES
1. **No financial figures unless confirmed** — payouts, users, volume, revenue. None of it.
2. **No superlatives without a named comparison** — "fastest" means nothing without "faster than X."
3. **No statistics without a source** — if you can't name it, don't quote it.
4. **No features outside the verified list** — when in doubt, it's not verified.
5. **When in doubt, write around it** — there is always an accurate way to say something compelling.

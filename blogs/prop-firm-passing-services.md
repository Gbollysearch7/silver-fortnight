# Prop Firm Passing Services: Why They Cost You Twice

**Prop firm passing services** are real, they are easy to find, and paying for one is the fastest way to lose both the service fee and the account it was meant to unlock. That is the short answer. If someone else trades your evaluation, you have breached the terms of service before your first funded trade, and the identity check at payout means the money was never coming to you anyway.

This post is for the trader with a challenge open in one tab and a "guaranteed pass" offer in the other. Read this before you pay anyone.

## What prop firm passing services actually sell

Two products dominate this corner of the market.

The first is plain **account sharing**. You hand your login credentials to a stranger, they trade your evaluation from their desk, and you get the account back once the profit target is hit. Some sellers dress it up as "account management". It is the same thing: a person you have never met trading an account registered in your name.

The second is the **"HFT pass" bot**. These services claim an automated system can hit your profit target in a handful of sessions, usually by exploiting price-feed latency or spraying high-frequency entries around news spikes. On any serious firm, that toolkit is a banned-practices bingo card. TradersYard, for example, explicitly bans arbitrage and latency exploits, news-trading abuse, and gambling-style behaviour.

Both products share one promise and one flaw. The promise: you skip the hard part. The flaw: the firm's entire business depends on knowing who is actually trading, and it checks.

## Prop firm account sharing breaks the one rule every firm enforces

Read any reputable firm's terms and you will find the same clause: the person who bought the evaluation must be the person trading it. This is not fine print. TradersYard's published banned list makes the position concrete. **Copy trading is banned.** Only **one challenge account may be connected at a time**. Why so strict? Because of what happens after you pass. No firm can responsibly copy signals from a trader whose actual trading it has never seen. A hired passer does not bend a rule. He poisons the entire relationship the evaluation exists to build.

## How firms detect a hired passer

Detection does not require magic. It requires records, and firms keep all of them.

Login forensics come first. Every session leaves a device fingerprint, an IP address, and a location. An account bought in one country that suddenly trades from another continent, or from a data-centre IP, raises an obvious question. New devices appearing at exactly the moment performance improves is not subtle either.

The second layer is behavioural, and it is the harder one to fake. Trading style is a signature: instrument choice, hold times, position sizing, session timing, reaction to losses. When the evaluation is traded one way and the funded account is traded a completely different way, the break is visible in the data. The passer's edge left with the passer.

And remember: at firms that ban VPN use outright, the tool a passing service uses to hide its traders is itself a violation, before a single trade is even examined.

## The KYC wall: the person who passed is not the person getting paid

Here is the part the sales pages never mention. Before any payout, firms verify identity through [KYC, or know-your-client checks](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/k/knowyourclient.asp): government ID, proof of identity, often a liveness check.

At TradersYard, payouts are processed only after KYC is complete. So the sequence looks like this: a stranger passes your evaluation, you request your first payout, and the firm now verifies that the person on the ID is the person behind the account. The account history it is about to pay out on was not produced by you, and the login and style records say so.

Even in the best case for the cheat, where nothing gets flagged early, you are now holding a funded account you did not earn. You are expected to trade at a level you never demonstrated, under real constraints like TradersYard's 40% consistency rule and daily drawdown limits. The market grades that honestly, every time.

## FAQ

### Do prop firm passing services actually work?

Some can hit a profit target on an evaluation. That was never the goal, though. The goal is withdrawing money from a funded account, and that requires surviving identity verification and conduct review. That is where the scheme collapses: the account history was not produced by the person on the ID.

### Is using a prop firm passing service illegal?

This isn't legal advice, and rules vary by country, but in most places it is a breach of contract rather than a criminal offence. Either way, the distinction will not comfort you: the firm's terms govern the account, breach ends it, the fee is forfeited, and no payout is owed.

### Can a firm really tell that someone else traded my account?

Yes. Firms hold login records, device fingerprints, IP locations, and complete trade histories. A change of trader shows up technically, through new devices and locations, and behaviourally, through a different trading style between the evaluation and funded phases.

### What are "HFT pass" bots, and are they allowed?

They are automated systems that exploit price-feed latency or news spikes to hit evaluation targets quickly. The tactics they rely on, arbitrage, latency exploits, and news-trading abuse, are banned practices at serious firms, TradersYard included.

### What happens to my money if I get caught?

The account is terminated, the challenge fee is forfeited, and any pending payout is refused. The passing-service fee is gone too. No service refunds you for being caught.

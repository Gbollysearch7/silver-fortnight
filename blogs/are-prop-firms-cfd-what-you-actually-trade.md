# Are Prop Firms CFD? What You Actually Trade in 2026

Are prop firms CFD? On the pricing side, mostly yes: the majority of forex prop firms quote their instruments as CFDs, contracts for difference on currency pairs, indices, metals, and crypto. But here is the twist most traders miss. On a modern prop firm evaluation, you are not trading real CFDs at all.

Your challenge account is a simulation funded with virtual money. The CFD pricing is the data layer that drives your charts and your fills, not a live position sitting at a broker. Nothing is routed to a liquidity provider in your name when you click buy.

That one distinction changes how you should judge a firm. This guide covers what a CFD actually is, how forex prop firms use CFD-style pricing, how futures prop firms differ, and what all of it means for your risk and your payouts.

## The Short Answer: CFD Pricing, Simulated Positions

A prop firm sells evaluations: prove you can trade profitably within a fixed set of rules, and the firm pays you a share of the profit you generate. If the model is new to you, read our full breakdown of [what a prop firm is](https://tradersyard.com/blog-posts/what-is-a-prop-firm) first.

The instruments you see inside the platform (EURUSD, gold, NAS100 and so on) are usually priced as CFDs, because CFD feeds are a cheap, flexible way to stream tradable quotes across hundreds of markets. The account itself, though, is simulated from your first trade to your last.

So when traders ask "do prop firms trade CFDs", the precise answer is: the pricing is CFD-style, but you are not holding a CFD with anyone. There is no contract between you and a broker, no margin call that can chase you, and no counterparty on the other side of your ticket.

## What a CFD Actually Is, and Why US Traders Cannot Touch One

A contract for difference is a leveraged over-the-counter derivative. You agree with a broker to exchange the difference between an asset's price when you open the trade and when you close it. You never own the underlying asset, as [Investopedia's CFD explainer](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/c/contractfordifferences.asp) sets out.

Three properties of real CFDs matter for this discussion:

- **They are OTC.** The contract exists only between you and your broker, and the broker sets the pricing and terms.
- **They are leveraged.** Regulators treat them as high risk; in the EU, ESMA capped retail CFD leverage at 30:1 on major currency pairs.
- **They are off-limits to US retail traders.** US regulation does not permit retail CFD trading, which is exactly why prop firms built for a US audience lean on futures instead.

If you want the trade-by-trade mechanics of the instrument itself, our [CFD trading step-by-step guide](https://tradersyard.com/blog-posts/cfd-trading-step-by-step-guide) walks through orders, margin, and position sizing in detail.

## Do Prop Firms Trade CFDs on Real Accounts? Not on Yours

When you buy a challenge at a CFD-style forex prop firm, you receive a demo account connected to a CFD price feed. Spreads, swaps, commissions, and leverage are all modelled inside the simulation. Every fill happens against the feed, not against a market.

This is how most of the industry works in 2026, and it is not a scandal. The evaluation exists to measure skill under realistic conditions, and a well-built simulation does exactly that job.

What the firm does on its own side is a separate question. Some firms replicate the signals of traders who pass into real corporate accounts. Others simply pay winners out of evaluation revenue. Either way, your side of the trade stays virtual.

For you, the consequences are mostly positive. You cannot lose more than your entry fee, you carry no counterparty risk, and there is no real margin account that can go negative.

## How Futures Prop Firms Are Different

Futures prop firms build their evaluations on exchange-traded contracts instead of OTC pricing. A futures contract is a standardised agreement that trades on a regulated exchange, as [Investopedia's futures guide](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/f/futurescontract.asp) explains, with prices coming from central venues such as [CME Group](https://www.cmegroup.com/).

That changes the texture of the data. You see one official price, real traded volume, and fixed tick sizes rather than broker-set spreads. Contract sizes are standardised too, so position sizing works in whole contracts rather than flexible lots.

The evaluation account is still simulated at almost every futures firm. The difference sits in the data layer underneath: exchange data instead of CFD quotes.

Because futures are fully legal for US retail traders, futures firms dominate the American market. ## Prop Firm Instruments Compared: CFD-Style vs Futures

| Feature | CFD-style forex prop firms | Futures prop firms |
|---|---|---|
| Pricing source | Broker-style CFD quotes | Exchange data (CME and others) |
| Instrument behind the feed | OTC derivatives | Exchange-traded contracts |
| Your actual position | Simulated, virtual funds | Simulated, virtual funds |
| Spreads and fees | Modelled by the firm | Exchange fees and commissions modelled |
| US retail access | Real CFDs are banned; simulated challenges remain accessible | Fully permitted |
| Typical markets | FX pairs, indices, metals, crypto | Index, energy, metals, rates futures |

Read the third row twice. Whatever the label says, your position is simulated in both models. The instrument type describes the data feed, not your exposure.

## What CFD Prop Trading Means for You in Practice

Three practical consequences follow once you understand that CFD prop trading runs on simulated accounts.

**First, spreads and commissions are modelled, so their quality varies.** A serious firm mirrors live conditions closely: spreads that widen at news, sensible swap charges, believable slippage. A careless firm hands you fantasy fills that fall apart the moment its rules meet real volatility.

**Second, you carry no counterparty risk.** You cannot owe money, face a margin call on a real position, or lose more than your entry fee. The financial risk in prop trading sits in the fee, not in the account.

**Third, payouts are based on simulated performance.** The firm pays you from its own revenue when you pass and trade well. That makes the firm's payment record and transparency the things to scrutinise, which is exactly the question we tackle in [are prop firms legit](https://tradersyard.com/blog-posts/are-prop-firms-legit).

Here is the stance worth holding: whether the pricing is CFD or futures matters far less than whether the firm's data, spreads, and rules mirror real market conditions. A trader drilled on honest data transfers to any market. A trader drilled on fantasy spreads has learned habits that will cost money anywhere real.

## FAQ

### Are prop firm accounts real money?

No. Evaluations across the industry run on simulated accounts with virtual funds. At TradersYard, every account is a demo account throughout, including the Funded Level. The payouts, however, are real money.

### Do prop firms trade CFDs with a real broker?

Not on your account. The CFD pricing you trade against is a data feed inside a simulation. Some firms may replicate trader signals in their own corporate accounts, but your positions never sit at a broker.

### Why do US prop firms prefer futures over CFDs?

US regulation bans retail CFD trading, while futures are fully legal and exchange-regulated, so firms built for American customers use futures data. Simulated, CFD-priced challenges are still offered to US residents because the account itself is a demo account, not a real CFD contract. This is a structural point, not legal advice, so check your own position with a qualified professional if you are unsure. TradersYard accepts US traders.

### Does simulated trading make the skills less real?

No, as long as the data is honest. If spreads, slippage, and news behaviour mirror live conditions, the discipline you build transfers directly. The rules of a good evaluation are often stricter than anything a retail broker imposes.

### What instruments can I trade at a prop firm?

CFD-style forex firms typically offer currency pairs, indices, metals, and crypto pricing. Futures firms offer index, energy, metals, and rates contracts on exchange data. Always check the firm's instrument list and data quality before paying an entry fee.

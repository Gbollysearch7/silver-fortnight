# Best Funded Prop Firms for Gold Traders (2026 Guide)

The best funded prop firms for gold are not the ones shouting about metals leverage. They are the ones whose rules you can actually survive while trading the most violent instrument most retail traders ever touch. Gold trading with prop firms fails for one specific reason: traders size XAUUSD like it is EURUSD, and gold does not move like EURUSD.

Gold is the account-killer of prop trading. Every rule a firm writes, daily drawdown, news windows, consistency requirements, interacts with gold differently than it does with a major currency pair. This guide covers both routes into a gold funded account, the sizing maths, and what to check in a firm before you trade a single ounce there.

## Gold Breaks Funded Accounts in Ways EURUSD Never Will

Three features of gold's volatility profile collide with prop firm rules harder than any major forex pair.

**Big daily dollar ranges.** Gold can cover a wide dollar range in a single session with no headline attached, and far more on a central bank day. Price does not drift, it lurches. A move that takes EURUSD a week can happen in gold before lunch.

**Violent news spikes.** Gold is a macro instrument. It reprices instantly on CPI, Fed decisions and payrolls because it trades against the dollar and real yields. The spike-and-reverse pattern around these releases is where stop-losses get skipped and daily limits get vaporised.

**Session gaps.** Spot gold closes over the weekend and reopens with whatever the world decided in between. A stop-loss does not protect you through a gap; you get filled at the reopening price.

None of this makes gold untradeable. It makes gold unforgiving of forex-calibrated habits. The traders who keep a **gold funded account** alive treat it as a different instrument class, not a spicy currency pair.

## Two Ways to Trade Gold With a Prop Firm: Spot XAUUSD or COMEX Futures

There are two distinct routes, and they live on different account types. If you have not settled that choice yet, read our breakdown of [forex vs futures prop firms](https://tradersyard.com/blog-posts/forex-vs-futures-prop-firms) first.

**Route one: spot gold (XAU/USD) on an FX-style account.** Gold is quoted like a currency pair and traded in lots, usually alongside forex majors on the same account. Leverage on metals is set by the firm and frequently differs from pair leverage.

**Route two: gold futures on a futures account.** COMEX gold futures trade on CME Group's exchange with a central order book: the full-size **GC contract covers 100 troy ounces** and the micro **MGC contract covers 10 troy ounces**. Contract specs are public on the [CME Group gold futures page](https://www.cmegroup.com/markets/metals/precious/gold.html). Dedicated [futures prop firms](https://tradersyard.com/blog-posts/futures-prop-firms) evaluate you on exchange-traded contracts with per-contract margin instead of leverage ratios.

| | Spot gold (XAUUSD) | GC futures | MGC futures |
|---|---|---|---|
| Contract size | 100 troy oz per standard lot | 100 troy oz | 10 troy oz |
| Value of a $1 move | $100 per standard lot | $100 per contract | $10 per contract |
| Minimum tick | Varies by firm's feed | $0.10 = $10 | $0.10 = $1 |
| Where it trades | OTC via your firm's price feed | COMEX (CME Group) | COMEX (CME Group) |
| Account type | FX-style funded account | Futures funded account | Futures funded account |

MGC gives you ten times the sizing granularity of GC, and granularity is exactly what gold demands.

## Position Sizing on Gold Is Not EURUSD Maths

Here is the trap. On EURUSD, one standard lot is roughly $10 per pip, and majors move in polite increments. On XAUUSD, one standard lot is **$100 for every $1 the metal moves**. A $10 gold move against a single lot is $1,000 gone. Gold produces moves that size can't shrug off.

Work the numbers backwards from your daily limit, not forwards from your confidence. All figures below are illustrative.

Say you trade a $50,000 account with a $2,000 daily loss limit and you risk $500 per trade, which is 1% of balance. On spot gold:

- Stop $5 away: $500 ÷ ($100 x 5) = **1.0 standard lot maximum**
- Stop $10 away: **0.5 lots maximum**
- Stop $20 away, sensible on a volatile day: **0.25 lots maximum**

On futures the same risk budget translates cleanly. A $5 stop on one GC contract risks $500, so you can hold exactly one. The same $5 stop on MGC risks $50 per contract, so you could scale between one and ten micros.

Notice what happened: a "normal" forex position of one lot with a 50-pip stop risks about $500 on EURUSD. The equivalent risk on gold often means a quarter of the size you instinctively want to trade. If a firm's [troy ounce](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/t/troyounce.asp) contract maths feels unfamiliar, that is your cue to trade micros until it does not.

## Daily Drawdown and News Rules Collide With Gold Hardest

A daily drawdown limit is calibrated against typical intraday movement. Gold's typical intraday movement is extreme, so the same percentage limit that feels roomy on forex feels like a tripwire on metals. One impulsive gold leg can consume a full day's allowance in minutes. Our guide to the [prop firm daily loss limit](https://tradersyard.com/blog-posts/prop-firm-daily-loss-limit-how-to-calculate-and-manage-it) covers the calculation in detail.

Timing matters too. At TradersYard the daily drawdown is equity-based and **resets at 00:00 UTC**. A late New York session gold flush lands hours before that reset, which means a bad afternoon and a bad evening share the same allowance. Plan your gold risk around the reset clock, not around your local trading day.

Then there are news rules. TradersYard restricts trading **10 minutes before and 5 minutes after high-impact news**, and the restriction always applies on funded accounts. On a quiet cross rate that window is a formality. On gold it is the entire event. CPI and Fed minutes are precisely when gold does its most violent repricing, so a gold-heavy strategy built on trading the number itself is structurally incompatible with most funded accounts. Trade the aftermath, not the spike.

And respect the weekend. Holding spot gold through a Friday close in a funded account is a decision to accept gap risk with rules attached. Futures reopen Sunday evening, spot reopens with the new week's pricing, and neither cares where your stop was.

## The Consistency Rule Punishes the One Big Gold Day

Gold traders have a signature pattern: grind modest results all week, then one trending gold session prints a monster. Consistency rules exist to flag exactly that shape.

TradersYard applies a **40% consistency rule**: your best single day's closed profit must be no more than 40% of your total closed profit. The maths is blunt. If your best gold day banked $2,000, you need at least $5,000 in total closed profit before that day sits within the rule.

Two practical fixes. First, keep gold position sizes inside your normal risk framework so no single session towers over the rest. Second, treat gold as one instrument in a rotation rather than the lottery ticket you pull out on trend days. The rule does not punish profit. It punishes profit that arrives as one spike, which is exactly how undisciplined gold trading pays out right before it takes everything back.

TradersYard has **no time limits** on challenges or funded accounts, so no clock forces you to swing big on one gold day. Spreading the same edge across ten sessions passes the rule; concentrating it into one does not.

## FAQ

### Can you trade gold with a prop firm?

Yes, through two routes: spot XAU/USD on FX-style funded accounts, or COMEX gold futures (GC and MGC contracts) on futures funded accounts. Confirm gold is on the firm's current instrument list before buying a challenge, because metals are not universally offered.

### Is XAUUSD harder to pass a challenge with than forex pairs?

Harder is the wrong word; less forgiving is accurate. One standard lot of XAUUSD moves $100 per $1 of price change, and gold routinely produces large intraday swings. The same sizing mistakes that cost you a little on EURUSD can end a challenge on gold in one session.

### What lot size should I use on gold in a funded account?

Work backwards from your stop distance and daily loss limit rather than picking a lot size first. As an illustration, risking $500 with a $10 stop on spot gold means 0.5 lots maximum. Micro futures (MGC, 10 troy ounces) offer the finest sizing control.

### What is the difference between GC and MGC gold futures?

GC is the full-size COMEX contract covering 100 troy ounces, where a $1 move is worth $100. MGC is the micro contract covering 10 troy ounces, worth $10 per $1 move. Both trade on CME Group's COMEX exchange with identical pricing.

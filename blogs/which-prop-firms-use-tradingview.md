You searched for which prop firms use TradingView because you want a list of names. ## Do Any Prop Firms Use TradingView? Yes, but "Use" Means Three Things

First, get the definition straight, because most of the confusion starts here. [TradingView](https://www.tradingview.com) is a charting and analysis platform built by TradingView Inc. It is not a broker and it is not a prop firm. No prop firm owns TradingView, and TradingView does not hand out funded accounts.

When a prop firm says it "uses TradingView", one of three things is true:

- **Native TradingView login.** You trade your challenge account directly inside TradingView's own interface, connected through a broker or trading-technology provider that TradingView supports.
- **Embedded TradingView charts.** The firm's own platform displays TradingView charting inside it, licensed through TradingView's charting libraries. You get the familiar candles and drawing tools, but not your TradingView account.
- **No real integration.** The marketing page shows TradingView-style screenshots, and you end up trading on something else entirely.

Firms rarely spell out which of the three they mean. That is not always deliberate, but the effect is the same: traders buy a challenge expecting their full TradingView setup and get a lookalike chart instead.

## How TradingView Integration With a Prop Firm Actually Works

TradingView does not connect to prop firms directly. It connects to **brokers and trading-technology providers**, and it publishes a list of the ones it supports inside the platform and on its website.

A prop firm that offers native TradingView access does it by routing your challenge account through one of those supported partners. You open TradingView, pick the broker from the trading panel, log in with the credentials the firm gave you, and your orders flow through the firm's infrastructure. This setup is most common among [futures prop firms](https://tradersyard.com/blog-posts/futures-prop-firms), because several futures brokers and technology providers sit on TradingView's supported list.

The embedded route is different. TradingView licenses its charting libraries to thousands of fintech products, so any platform can build TradingView-grade charts into its own front end. That is legitimate and often well executed. But your paid TradingView plan, saved layouts, custom Pine Script indicators, watchlists, and alerts do not follow you there.

This distinction is the whole game. When traders ask what prop firms let you use TradingView, most of them really mean: **can I keep my layouts, my indicators, and my alerts?** Only native login gives you that. Embedded charts give you the look, not the account.

## Which Prop Firms Work With TradingView: The Three Levels Compared

Here is the full picture in one table. When you evaluate any firm, place it on this scale before you pay.

| Level | What you actually get | How to spot it | The catch |
|---|---|---|---|
| Native TradingView login | Your real TradingView account: layouts, indicators, alerts, Pine Script | The firm names its broker, and that broker appears in TradingView's supported list | Some TradingView features need a paid plan; execution quality depends on the broker |
| Embedded TradingView charts | Familiar charting inside the firm's own platform | Charts look like TradingView, but you never log in at tradingview.com | No saved layouts, no custom indicators, no sync with your existing setup |
| No integration | The firm's own charts or a separate third-party platform | TradingView appears in screenshots or ad copy only | You learn a new platform while also trying to pass a challenge |

None of these levels is automatically bad. A firm's own platform can be excellent, especially when it is built around the firm's actual rules. The problem is only ever mismatched expectations: paying for level one and receiving level two or three.

## How to Verify a Firm Supports TradingView in Five Minutes

You do not need a list of firms. You need a repeatable check that works on any firm, this year and next. It takes about five minutes.

**Read the platform page properly.** Not the homepage banner, the actual platform or FAQ page. Precise wording matters. "Trade directly on TradingView" points to native login. "TradingView-style charts" or "powered by TradingView charting" points to embedded charts. No mention at all outside screenshots points to level three.

**Ask support one exact question.** Write this, word for word: "Can I log into my challenge account at tradingview.com using my own TradingView profile, and which broker do I select in the trading panel?" A firm with real integration answers with a specific broker name. A vague answer, or a pivot to how great their charts are, tells you it is embedded at best.

**Cross-check TradingView's own broker list.** Open TradingView, open the trading panel, and search for the broker the firm named. If it is not there, native login is not happening, whatever the sales page says.

**Trial the platform before you pay if you can.** Some firms offer a free trial or demo environment, and it is the fastest way to see exactly what you would be trading on. Availability varies a lot between firms, so check our guide to [prop firm demo account platforms](https://tradersyard.com/blog-posts/prop-firm-demo-account-practice-best-platforms) before assuming one exists.

Run those four checks and you will know more about a firm's TradingView support than most of its affiliates do.

## The Trade-Off: Familiar Charting vs Execution Features

Insisting on TradingView is a reasonable preference. Familiar charting saves real time, and your [technical analysis](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/t/technicalanalysis.asp) workflow lives in muscle memory: the drawing tools, the indicator stack, the alert routine you have refined for years.

But be honest about what TradingView does not do. It shows you charts and executes orders. It does not natively track the rules that actually end challenges: daily drawdown distance, trailing drawdown levels, consistency requirements, or news-trading windows. A purpose-built prop platform can display your distance to breach in real time, right next to the chart. On native TradingView, you are often tracking those numbers yourself in a separate dashboard.

There is also the data question. Charting and market data are separate costs in this industry, and futures data in particular is often billed separately depending on the setup. Before you buy, confirm who pays for the datafeed, because a cheap challenge with a monthly data bill on top is not a cheap challenge.

The senior-trader take: pick the firm on rules, payouts, and trust first, platform second. A perfect chart cannot save you from a drawdown rule you did not understand, and passing is mostly about process anyway. ## FAQ

### Do any prop firms use TradingView?

Yes. Some offer native TradingView login through a supported broker, many more embed TradingView charting inside their own platforms, and some have no integration at all. The label "supports TradingView" covers all three, so verify which level a firm actually offers before paying.

### Can I use my own TradingView account with a prop firm?

Only with a native integration. The firm must route accounts through a broker or technology provider on TradingView's supported list, and you connect through TradingView's trading panel. If the firm cannot name that broker, your personal layouts and indicators will not carry over.

### Which prop firms support TradingView natively?

We do not publish name lists, because integrations change and stale lists mislead traders into paying the wrong firm. The reliable answer is the five-minute check in this guide: read the platform page, ask support the exact login question, and confirm the broker on TradingView's own list.

### Is TradingView a broker or a prop firm?

Neither. TradingView is a charting and analysis platform operated by TradingView Inc. Trading happens through connected brokers, and funding comes from prop firms. It is one tool in the stack, not the counterparty.

### Do prop firms with TradingView charge extra for market data?

Sometimes. Charting and market data are separate services, and futures data especially can carry its own cost depending on the firm's setup. Always confirm whether the datafeed is included before you buy. TradersYard includes the datafeed and platform in its single entry fee.

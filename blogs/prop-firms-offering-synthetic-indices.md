# Prop Firms Offering Synthetic Indices: Read This First

Yes, prop firms offering synthetic indices exist. You can find funded accounts built around volatility-style synthetic markets if you go looking for them. But they occupy a small, specific corner of the funded trading industry, and that is not an accident: the way synthetic indices are built collides head-on with the way most evaluation firms model risk.

This guide gives you the straight answer. What synthetic indices actually are, why the mainstream of the prop industry will not touch them, what to scrutinize hard if you buy a synthetic indices funded account anyway, and the alternative that most synthetic traders adapt to faster than they expect.

## Synthetic Indices Are a Price Feed, Not a Market

Synthetic indices are algorithm-generated price streams designed to simulate the statistical behaviour of real markets: volatility clusters, trends, ranges, spikes and crashes. The algorithm produces ticks around the clock, every day of the week. No sessions, no weekend gaps, no economic calendar, no earnings surprises.

That constant volatility is exactly why traders like them: you always know roughly how much movement to expect, and the market never closes on you mid-trade.

Here is the part that matters more. Each synthetic index is proprietary to the specific broker that generates it. It does not trade on any exchange. There is no underlying asset, no interbank market, no futures contract referencing it. The index exists in exactly one place: the servers of the company that invented it.

Compare that with EUR/USD or a major equity index. Dozens of venues and data providers quote the same instrument, and you can cross-check any price against an independent source in seconds. A synthetic index has no independent source. It sits closer to a proprietary [over-the-counter product](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/o/otc.asp) than to a market, except even most OTC instruments reference something external. Synthetics reference nothing but their own algorithm.

That is not automatically sinister. But it changes everything about who you have to trust.

## Why Mainstream Prop Firms Will Not Touch Them

If you have read up on [what is a prop firm](https://tradersyard.com/blog-posts/what-is-a-prop-firm), you know the evaluation model runs on data. The firm sets drawdown limits, profit targets and consistency rules, then measures your trading against a price feed it treats as neutral ground.

Synthetic indices break that neutrality in three ways.

**The feed belongs to one broker.** A prop firm offering synthetics has to source prices from the company that generates them. The firm cannot switch providers, compare competing feeds or audit ticks against a reference market, because no reference market exists. Its entire product now depends on a data source it neither controls nor verifies.

**Disputes become unresolvable.** On a futures instrument there is an exchange record: time and sales, official settlement prices, regulated market surveillance. If a trader claims a bad tick breached their drawdown, everyone can check the tape. On a synthetic index, the only record is the generator's own log. The referee owns the scoreboard.

**Risk cannot be benchmarked externally.** Evaluation firms model trader risk against known market behaviour: how far an index moves on a news day, what slippage looks like at the open. Synthetic volatility is whatever the algorithm says it is, and the algorithm can be changed by its owner at any time. You cannot build a stable risk model on top of that.

None of this means every synthetic offering is dishonest. It means the structure removes the independent verification the rest of the industry is built on. That is why the list of prop firms offering synthetic indices stays short, and why established evaluation firms keep their distance.

## How to Vet a Synthetic Indices Prop Firm Before You Pay

If you still want a synthetic indices funded account, go in with your eyes open. The general due diligence covered in [are prop firms legit](https://tradersyard.com/blog-posts/are-prop-firms-legit) applies double here, because you are stacking an unverifiable market on top of the usual counterparty questions.

Three things deserve hard scrutiny before any entry fee leaves your account.

**Who controls the price feed.** If the same corporate group generates the index, runs the platform and approves the withdrawals, every layer of the trade depends on a single counterparty. That is a concentration of trust no trader should accept casually. Ask directly: who generates the feed, is it audited by anyone outside the group, and what is the documented process when a price is disputed?

**Payout track record.** Look for dated, verifiable payout proofs stretching back through different periods, not a wall of anonymous screenshots. A firm that has paid consistently for years has a reputation to lose. A firm with a few months of history and recycled testimonials does not.

**Rule transparency.** The complete rulebook should be public before you pay: exact drawdown definitions, consistency requirements, banned strategies and the dispute process. Vague rules combined with an unverifiable feed is the worst pairing in funded trading, because the firm can always construct a reason after the fact.

If any one of the three fails, walk away. No chart setup is good enough to compensate for a counterparty you cannot check.

## The Alternative: Real Index Products

Here is what most synthetic traders have not been told: the skills transfer. If you learned to trade algorithm-generated volatility, you learned to read momentum, respect spikes and manage risk in fast conditions. Real index markets reward exactly those instincts.

[Index futures](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/i/indexfutures.asp) are the natural landing spot. Yes, they have sessions, and yes, they gap. In exchange you get pricing set on a regulated exchange, an official record of every trade, and an educational ecosystem far deeper than anything built around proprietary synthetics.

| | Synthetic indices | Index futures |
|---|---|---|
| Price source | One broker's algorithm | Regulated exchange |
| Externally verifiable | No | Yes, public time and sales |
| Trading hours | Around the clock, every day | Most of each weekday, in sessions |
| Gaps and news | None | Yes, must be managed |
| Dispute record | Generator's own logs | Official exchange data |
| Funded account availability | A small niche | Broad and established |

The adjustment is real. You will need to learn session opens, economic releases and gap risk. That is a learnable set of skills, and it comes with something synthetics can never offer: every price you trade against exists outside anyone's private server.

Futures give you exchange-grade transparency and centralised volume data.

## FAQ

### Do any prop firms offer synthetic indices funded accounts?

Yes, a small number do. Synthetic indices funded accounts exist, but they remain a niche within the industry because the price feeds are proprietary to single brokers and cannot be independently verified.

### Why do most prop firms avoid synthetic indices?

The feed belongs to one company, there is no external reference market, and disputes cannot be settled against an exchange record. That structure conflicts with how evaluation firms model, measure and verify trader risk.

### Are synthetic indices rigged?

There is no way to know from the outside, and that is precisely the problem. The algorithm and its logs sit with the company that generates the index, so you are trusting the operator rather than verifying the market.
